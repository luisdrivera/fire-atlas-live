Harmonized Satellite Intelligence (MODIS + VIIRS) and Monitoring in the Caribbean

Method & limitations

Method

  • Data: NASA FIRMS active-fire detections (MODIS and VIIRS) for the Caribbean. Low-confidence detections are excluded.
  • Before 2012: monthly MODIS counts are multiplied by a per-territory calibration factor (shown on screen for each territory).
  • From 2012: MODIS and VIIRS detections are fused and de-duplicated.
  • Live mode: queries NASA FIRMS near-real-time data and fuses sensors by de-duplicating detections at roughly 1 km and one day. This is de-duplication, not calibration. Live detections are kept only within about 0.2 degrees of cells where the historical record has detections for the selected territory, so live and historical counts cover the same area; fires in places with no historical detections are not shown.

Limitations

  • The series changes definition in 2012 (scaled MODIS before, fused MODIS + VIIRS after), so long-term trends across that year should be read with caution.
  • The reported correlation (r = 0.9923) is computed on monthly totals during the overlap period. Seasonality inflates monthly correlations, and it is not an out-of-sample test.
  • One factor per territory is used. It does not separate day from night and has not been tested on held-out data.
  • Counts are satellite detections, not individual fires.
  • Alert levels compare the current rate with the historical distribution for the same month. They are not forecasts.
  • "Simulate Alert" generates synthetic data for demonstration only.

For an independent validation study (La Española and Mato Grosso, trained 2018-2022, tested on 2023-2024), see https://fire-atlas-nu.vercel.app/
